### Private notes, custody and compatibility

The approved [N1–N5 contract](milestone-5-pr5-plan.md#decisions-before-implementation) and [ADR-002](../trainer-notes-settlement-adr.md) are deliberately narrow:

- The fixed path is `workspaces/{wid}/privateTrainerNotes/{clientUid}`. Exact current author/subject authority and immutable author/subject membership and global lifecycle revisions must match. Workspace ownership alone supplies no access. Direct Firestore note get/list/write remain denied, including to the author.
- Withdrawal leaves the record in inaccessible backend custody and the slot occupied. A replacement author cannot overwrite or consume it; return of the previous identity does not automatically restore historical access. A future reassignment writer must atomically advance relationship revisions. Raw Admin edits that bypass these invariants are outside the contract.
- Notes do not join the assignment-artifact guard and do not block membership revocation. Save reads the authority records that withdrawal changes: Save-first may commit before later denial; withdrawal-first denies Save. Already-disclosed content cannot be recalled. Entry/foreground/reload/Save recheck the subject; no new instant target-revocation listener is claimed.
- Unsent drafts are memory-only and clear on background/scope loss. Submitted exact text, nonce and original binding remain encrypted in the existing one-command UID envelope until validated durable settlement. Unavailable identity, damaged storage or unproved history can leave this content retained indefinitely in practice. Synthetic approval is neither a real-data retention duration nor permission for permanent personal-data custody.
- Retirement follows durable full pending proof, then compacts only the note's last-settled record to a tagged content-free marker. It carries UID, derived command identity/hash/kind/source/outcome and actual resolver decision time where applicable; no body, nonce, original command, raw key, client or workspace selector. Hashes are commitments, not anonymization. Compaction is not physical secure erasure.
- The seven older variants keep their original full proof forms and hashes. Upgraded readers accept legacy forms; unsupported old readers lock safely. A compact marker cannot substitute for pending proof, cross UIDs or coexist with pending work consuming that key. Storage ambiguity never permits reset/discard/replacement.

### Known assignment-index Rules limitation

[Current Rules](../../firebase/firestore.rules) authorize trainer discovery-index reads using the requesting active account, workspace, exact trainer membership and index identity/trainer binding. They **do not check the indexed client's current membership/relationship**. An otherwise eligible trainer may therefore still read an existing index after that client's membership changes. These indexes hold discovery metadata, not snapshot prescriptions or note bodies; that does not make historical metadata policy accepted.

PR 4 explicitly regression-tests and retains this limitation. It rejects every target with a retained account-owned assignment header or matching workspace index, including terminal artifacts, and serializes publication/removal through workspace `membershipRevision`. It neither repairs the index predicate nor supports removal of assignment-bearing clients. Milestone 6 source-catalog/template work must not query these indexes or enable assignment. Milestone 7 or any broader revocation/history slice needs an explicit access policy and corresponding Rules/lifecycle proof first.
