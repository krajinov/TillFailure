# Milestone 5 prerequisite: own-command outcome resolution

Status: **protocol and initial compatibility scope approved by Josip on 2026-10-21; emulator prerequisite implemented for review.** See the [implementation report](milestone-5-command-resolution-report.md) for evidence and remaining gates. Revocation execution/UI, production, deployment, destructive discard, identity repair and privileged overrides remain excluded.

The approval includes all six current protected variants, own minimal outcomes after workspace authority loss, covered absent-command fencing, durable proof before retirement, explicit Resolve confirmation and the documented legacy/identity/storage limits. References to proposed mechanics below describe this approved contract; the future seventh revocation variant remains unimplemented and separately gated.
Inspection date: **2026-10-21**, Europe/Sarajevo.
Baseline: `ce3bde2ed177c873c5b0f55c82949b8f70d18e5a`, branch `docs/milestone-5-pr4-revocation-plan`. The three existing uncommitted planning files were preserved and revised; this document is an additional untracked planning file. No application builds/tests were run.

## Recommendation and readiness

Add one **own-command resolver** that, for an authenticated eligible account and one exact original command, either projects an existing terminal receipt or atomically creates an immutable **not-applied fence at the existing receipt path**. Every business writer must treat any document at that path as consumed command identity. The business transaction and resolver transaction therefore compete on the same document; there is no separate lookup whose absence is treated as non-commit.

Separate two permissions:

- **Business execution/replay:** retains each operation's current account/workspace/membership/ownership/assignment and input authorization. A previously successful command cannot restore a role or authorize a later mutation.
- **Own outcome resolution:** approved permission to receive only the terminal decision for the caller's exact key/kind/payload, or foreclose its future execution, without current workspace membership. It grants no workspace, roster, client/profile/history or general receipt-read access.

Implement and prove this as a **separate emulator-only prerequisite slice before revocation UI/backend enablement**. Cover all six currently protected mutation variants, then require the proposed seventh (membership revocation) to use the same contract. Reuse `lifecycleCommands`, `InvitationLedger`, the encrypted UID envelope, native freeze/issuance locks and clean-departure coordinator. There is no new recovery queue, database, account-switch mechanism or privileged support resolver.

Josip's [approved revocation boundary](milestone-5-pr4-plan.md#approved-narrow-mvp-policy) stands. He **did not accept potentially indefinite lock caused solely by loss of workspace membership as finished behavior**. This prerequisite removes that authority dependency for supported, fully bound commands with intact records/identity. It does not claim to resolve damaged storage, missing identity, corrupt receipts or unproved legacy history.

Invitation receipts currently store `{schemaVersion:1, commandKind, callerUid, invitationId, workspaceId, requestHash, result, committedAt}`; their exact result keys are `expiresAtMillis, invitationId, status, tokenVersion, workspaceId`. Onboarding/profile use different result keys and different hash construction. The resolver needs explicit per-kind adapters, not a generic cast or reuse of `InvitationJson.validReply` against a differently shaped response.

The old handlers additionally compare receipts with **current** resource state, such as current invitation version/expiry, active accepted membership or current account revision. Those comparisons help their business replay boundary; they are not requirements for disclosing a minimal historical own outcome. Proposed resolver integrity checks instead validate the immutable receipt's schema, path/caller/kind/hash, exact allowlists, original request relationships and supported policy bounds. They must not re-create the authority-loss dependency by querying a live client profile or inviter. A malformed/unsupported receipt is not projected or repaired.

## Identity and disclosure boundary

**Proposed resolver eligibility:** fresh verified, non-anonymous, enabled Auth under the original UID, including current Auth lookup/token-revocation checks, and an existing schema-current active global account with valid lifecycle revision and current Auth/account email binding. Derive UID from Auth; the body cannot name another caller. Transactionally read that account with the command record so global account lifecycle changes serialize with the resolution decision. Do not require a workspace, membership reference, role, assignment, catalog entitlement or invitation inviter to remain active.

| Current condition | Proposed result |
|---|---|
| Workspace membership revoked; workspace suspended; caller no longer owner/trainer; inviter lost authority | Own minimal resolution still allowed if global eligibility/binding hold. Business mutation remains denied by the normal endpoint. No role/workspace is reopened. |
| Target of caller's revocation becomes revoked/disabled | Does not remove the initiating caller's own outcome permission. Never read/return target profile or use target account state as an outcome grant. |
| Caller Auth disabled/deleted, token invalid/revoked, unverified/anonymous, wrong UID | Deny resolver; no write/settlement based on the error. Do not enable Auth, use a cached session or substitute an operator. A refreshed Auth check cannot be atomic with external Auth deletion; an already authorized response may finish, but it contains no role/content grant. |
| Global account disabled/deleted/missing/unsupported/malformed | Deny; no account repair or temporary enablement to retrieve a receipt. A concurrent Firestore disable forces transaction re-evaluation. Identity/account recovery remains separate. |
| Invitation accept never bootstrapped a global account | Under this conservative first resolver policy, Auth alone is insufficient. Ordinary authorized acceptance may still succeed; otherwise recovery can remain locked. An Auth-only bootstrap outcome permission would be a separate identity-policy extension, not silently inferred here. |
| Another account signs in on the device | It cannot read, resolve, retire or rebind the original pending slot. Native UID/pin/freeze/epoch protections still apply. |

Request: one allowlisted command kind, original key, supported binding-format version and its exact original request descriptor. No caller UID, arbitrary path, receipt ID, batch, list, cursor, target-account lookup or arbitrary command kind is accepted. Workspace/target IDs in the descriptor are hash inputs, **not** resource access selectors. Validate length/types/segment/domain constraints without querying those resources. Onboarding/profile descriptors contain the caller's original submitted fields for hash verification; they are never echoed, logged, written to a new history collection or used as update instructions by this endpoint.

For new acceptance preparation, persist `acceptanceTokenDigest=SHA-256(original bearer)` as lowercase 64-character hex and binding version in the **same encrypted pending slot before any issuance**, alongside existing metadata. This matches the existing server's digest encoding. It is a one-way commitment to a high-entropy token, not a bearer accepted by the business endpoint; raw tokens remain process-only. Resolution sends the digest, not the bearer. Ordinary acceptance still needs the original bearer. Hashing and canonicalization must match each existing writer exactly, including invitation email lowercasing, absent versus null fields, integer bounds, nested keys, Unicode and the policy-version inputs for onboarding/profile. Freeze these formats with backend/Kotlin/Swift boundary test vectors; do not change historical hashes to a new generic formula. Proposed revocation gets an explicit format before implementation.

The server computes the request hash from the validated descriptor; it does not trust an asserted `neverCommitted` flag or accept a client hash as proof of absence. The client independently derives the expected hash from its protected original command. For a missing record, the server cannot prove there was an earlier network submission: the bounded permission is to foreclose **only this caller's key**. A caller can at worst consume an unused own key, never perform business work or consume someone else's identity. The product exposes this only for its protected pending command, with explicit resolve intent. This property, rather than a claim to authenticate local storage remotely, is part of the approved authority decision.

### Minimal terminal response

Exactly these fields; no nested original receipt or optional business DTO:

| Field | Validation / meaning |
|---|---|
| `protocolVersion` | Exactly the supported outcome-projection version, initially 1 |
| `commandKind` | Exact allowlisted kind of the protected original command |
| `commandId` | Digest derived from the current UID and original key; binds the response without echoing the key |
| `requestHashVersion` | Exact supported per-kind binding format |
| `requestHash` | Exact canonical request fingerprint matching the protected original payload |
| `outcome` | `applied`, `not-applied-conflict`, or `not-applied-fenced`; never inferred from HTTP error codes |
| `decidedAtMillis` | Valid server decision timestamp from the immutable receipt/fence, within supported timestamp bounds; not a grant/retention expiry |

`applied` means that particular command's business transaction committed, not that its historical invitation/profile/membership state is current. `not-applied-conflict` represents a supported immutable negative receipt (currently profile conflict; future revocation conflict). `not-applied-fenced` means this key was foreclosed with proven no earlier business commit under the coverage conditions below. No workspace name/ID, target/client ID, profile/name/locale/time zone, email, invitation ID/version/expiry, account revision, role, bearer, delivery status or history is returned. Next navigation/read requires fresh normal authorization.

Malformed requests can receive a fixed input error. Wrong identity receives fixed authentication/access guidance. Foreign/mismatched/corrupt/unsupported receipts and unverifiable legacy absence share `outcome-unavailable`, without exposing record existence, corruption location, caller/target state or raw backend errors. Rate/service/storage failures are nonterminal retry guidance. No error is a settlement acknowledgment.

## Atomic terminalization and fencing

### Records and invariant

Reuse the **same** `lifecycleCommands/C` path used by every covered business command. Keep existing schema-1 positive/profile-conflict receipts immutable. Add the approved terminal record with an intentionally distinct schema:

`{schemaVersion:2, commandKind:"own-command-fence", protocolVersion:1, callerUid, originalCommandKind, requestHashVersion, requestHash, outcome:"not-applied-fenced", decidedAt}`.

The document path binds caller/key; no raw payload, bearer or client profile is stored. Exact validation includes field allowlist, timestamp and path-derived command ID. The fence consumes **the whole caller/key**, not just the supplied kind/hash. A later invocation with another payload/kind must fail, not treat the fence as a missing/mismatched success record and overwrite it. A correct matching caller receives only the minimal projection. Unsupported schema or conflicting content stays immutable and unresolved; no overwrite, merge-upgrade or repair.

Invariant: **every business-effect transaction reads C, creates its receipt atomically with those effects, and makes no business writes if C already exists.** A recognized matching success/conflict returns a decision; a fence, unknown schema or conflicting binding also forbids business effects. There is no lease expiry, timeout-based stealing, separate cancellation collection, pending-state TTL or eventually written receipt. Firestore read/create conflicts decide ordering.

### Resolver transaction

1. Enforce named loopback demo environment, fresh original-UID identity, descriptor validation and bounded request admission. Construct C internally. Read current global account and C before writes; no workspace/client/invitation enumeration or reads are needed.
2. **C exists:** validate its exact kind-specific receipt or fence and original binding. Project `applied` / supported conflict / fence. Return an existing decision without rewriting it, changing timestamps or inspecting current resource status. Wrong binding/shape denies with no terminal proof.
3. **C absent:** require the server's verified writer/receipt-history coverage for this command namespace/generation and configured terminalization budget. Atomically `create` the fence at C, with its budget charge. Return `not-applied-fenced` only after that commit. If coverage is uncertain, return unavailable and retain local work; mere absence is not evidence.
4. Transaction retries reload **C and account**, up to three attempts within a bounded Firestore-work deadline. If another transaction won, validate/project that winner. If the service times out or the commit reply is lost, outcome remains unknown locally; retry this exact resolution. Never issue a new business key as recovery. The resolver does no business mutation regardless of transaction attempt count.

### Required race outcomes

| Ordering / race | Required proof |
|---|---|
| Business transaction commits first; response lost | Its effects and immutable receipt both exist. Resolver returns its validated applied/conflict decision; it cannot replace the receipt with a fence. |
| Resolver commits first; original transaction already read C absent | Original transaction conflicts/retries; it must observe the fence and perform no business effects. Its earlier authorization or in-memory delta cannot override C. |
| Resolver arrives before a delayed original invocation starts | Fence already exists at C. The delayed invocation either fails its normal live authorization or reads C and is blocked. No effects after the caller receives a fenced decision. |
| Same-key resolution repeated/concurrent | Exactly one immutable winner at C. Replays do not increment business counters or terminalization budget twice. |
| Changed payload or command kind under same key | Existing record is never replaced; mismatch gives redacted unavailable. Even a differently bound invocation cannot bypass a fence's whole-key protection. |
| Business durable conflict versus resolution | Either its existing immutable negative receipt wins or fence wins; both prevent this key producing later effects. Do not convert ordinary revision errors into negative receipts. |
| Outage, retry exhaustion, identity/global-account loss during work | No terminal local settlement from the error. A committed fence/receipt may still exist; exact retry later can recover it if identity permits. |

No poll count, clock interval, request cancellation, generic authorization rejection or client assertion proves non-commit. **A fence proves no future commit; no earlier commit additionally requires atomic receipt coverage and preserved history.** These are separate obligations.

## Receipt integrity and legacy coverage

Receipts are server-owned behind existing mobile default denial; resolution is a guarded mediator, never a direct Rules get/list grant. A request hash is binding/corruption checking, **not a MAC or evidence against a malicious Admin**. Validate schema/allowlists/types/path/caller/kind/hash plus per-kind result relationships before projection. Reject injected fields, wrong IDs, impossible revisions/statuses/timestamps or missing binding. Do not repair receipts from current client data. A structurally plausible privileged forgery is outside this trust boundary; operational access/audit/backup integrity remains a release requirement. No new receipt-signing key infrastructure is presumed.

For legacy invitation accept/revoke, the stored original expiry is checked for valid type/range and internal consistency; it need not equal the invitation's later expiry. Acceptance's original workspace is server-resolved and absent from its request hash; require stored workspace/result consistency but return no workspace information. Known trusted-writer provenance is essential. If a particular legacy shape cannot be validated without missing evidence, it stays unsupported, rather than exposing a resource or guessing that its status matches today.

| Legacy condition | Safe handling |
|---|---|
| Intact supported legacy receipt and complete original payload binding | Per-kind adapter can project its historical positive/negative outcome without current membership. Do not rewrite the receipt or require current profile revision/inviter/membership. |
| Legacy acceptance after process death with only ID/version/key | Cannot reconstruct the server request hash: the pending slot deliberately persisted no bearer or digest. Do not infer original bearer from matching invitation ID/version or fetch a token/hash from internal invitation as a substitute. Reopen the original link to reconstruct binding; an existing receipt will detect a mismatched token. Without that evidence, retain locked work. This is a missing-binding limitation, not a membership permission requirement. |
| Legacy acceptance with reopened but unproved token and no receipt | Do not label a fabricated digest the original submission. No default absent-legacy terminalization; retain pending work until separately audited identity/binding/history proof is available. |
| Legacy receipt absent, history not proved intact | No `not-applied` outcome. An old business commit could have had its receipt deleted/restored away. Do not create a fictitious positive or negative result, reseed the dataset or replace the local key. |
| Existing fence under older app/server | Older business handlers reject its schema and cannot produce effects; older clients may remain pending until an upgraded resolver-capable client is used. Unsupported local new fields/versions must lock, never drop a pending payload. |

**New-command absence coverage:** recommend a server-declared, fresh unpredictable **protocol-generation prefix on newly issued idempotency keys** within existing key validation limits: `or1-{generationId}-{UUID}`, with a server-generated 32-lowercase-hex generation ID and the existing UUID command nonce. Use one persisted generation for the controlled demo dataset; do not regenerate it at each process restart. Enable it only after auditing receipt-atomic writers and retaining all records for that generation. The generation starts after cutover; new applications persist the original prefixed key and full binding before issuance. The prefix is a namespace/coverage marker, not a credential or authority. Clients cannot declare an arbitrary generation covered; the resolver accepts absent-record terminalization only for trusted configured coverage. Record coverage loss on reset/import/repair and disable absent-record resolution if provenance is uncertain. Never rekey an already pending legacy command to obtain this treatment.

Normal writer guards must reserve the `or1-` prefix and reject unsupported/retired generations without effects, rather than treating an unknown prefix as a fresh legacy key. Deploy those guards to every shared-namespace writer before issuing new-generation keys. Existing genuinely legacy keys retain their old receipt-based replay rules and no absent-record coverage by default. In-flight older writers already reject a present fence; retaining all covered receipts/fences is still mandatory while any older writer can run. Generation format alone is not evidence of intact receipt history.

For intact legacy receipts, positive/conflict projection can be useful without claiming old absence coverage. Supporting **absent** legacy keys requires a separate audited dataset-history decision; default is disabled. This keeps the first prerequisite independent of a universal legacy migration. Production remains disabled, so an emulator coverage generation is not a production-retention policy.

**Retention/key reuse invariant:** no covered command receipt or fence may be expired/deleted/replaced while any accepted invocation with that key can run or retry. Unknown namespace/generation keys must never fall back to new execution after history is removed. Preserve consumed-key barriers in backup/export/restore; disable service rather than combine old receipts with newer effects or vice versa. A future bounded-retention design must prove permanent key retirement across *all* writers before compaction. This proposal selects no number of days, TTL, cleanup job or approved indefinite-retention policy; retention/operations approval remains required before deployment. The emulator prerequisite retains its test records and does not implement cleanup.

## Abuse controls and bounded work

One exact deterministic C point read; no enumeration/batches/existence-probing API. Require current eligible identity before record access. Canonical hash validation and strict input size bounds occur before constructing references; caller UID and key must satisfy existing domain contracts. Log only redacted outcome categories and unrelated correlation IDs, not keys/hashes, bodies, profile fields, email, invitation/token material or receipt contents. No raw exception reason escapes.

Reuse the fixture's separation between bounded request/replay throttling and admission for a new immutable record. Rate limits apply to successes, misses, mismatches and repeated resolution, preventing a cheap digest oracle or unbounded storage consumption. In addition, require a finite trusted per-caller/per-generation **new-fence budget** before enabling the demo endpoint; no client-supplied cap, default unlimited budget or receipt enumeration. Existing decision replay does not consume new-fence budget, so exhaustion cannot itself prevent returning an already committed outcome. Infrastructure/quota failures produce no terminal acknowledgment and do not erase local work.

Proposed per effect transaction: at most four fixed documents (account, C, terminalization budget, trusted coverage/config record if stored), at most two writes (new fence and its admission count), at most three transaction attempts and a five-second Firestore-work deadline after Auth. A separate bounded request counter transaction may precede it, as in invitations; count it explicitly in implementation tests. No profile, workspace, membership, account inventory or invitation scan. Bound descriptor/response bytes and index/request costs; unsupported configuration fails closed. Coverage/config can instead be immutable service configuration but must remain stable across retries/restarts and be included in rollout provenance.

## Protected native settlement and user states

Extend [InvitationLedger](../../shared/src/commonMain/kotlin/com/delminiusapps/tillfailure/invitations/InvitationClient.kt) and the [existing encrypted envelope](../../shared/src/commonMain/kotlin/com/delminiusapps/tillfailure/persistence/PersistencePrototype.kt); do not replace them. Add versioned binding metadata and a bounded terminal-outcome proof, retaining the one pending slot. The Android [adapter](../../shared/src/androidMain/kotlin/com/delminiusapps/tillfailure/firebase/AndroidFirebaseSpikeClient.kt) and Apple [Kotlin wrapper](../../shared/src/iosMain/kotlin/com/delminiusapps/tillfailure/firebase/IosFirebaseBridge.kt)/[Swift transport](../../iosApp/iosApp/FirebaseNativeBridge.swift) must use the same strict parser and issuance/settlement locks. No arbitrary endpoint selector or receipt path reaches native callers.

Recommended durable sequence for **all matching positive/conflict/fenced settlement paths**, including ordinary native mutation completions:

The local proof has two explicit source variants: the resolver's exact seven-field response, or an existing strictly validated normal mutation acknowledgment bound to its captured original request. Existing normal replies do not echo every hash/timestamp field; do not fabricate a server-echoed fingerprint or decision time for them. Store their locally computed original binding plus the validated kind-specific acknowledgment and source version. Strengthen missing request/derived-ID checks where needed. Both variants prove terminal settlement under the trusted server contract and use the same durable sequence; a resolver response is never fed into the old business-reply parser.

1. Match current pinned UID, original pending kind/key/full payload binding, request/response protocol, command ID and hash. Validate the terminal response or kind-specific normal receipt reply; never accept an error as a terminal decision. A resolver reply does not need the old active workspace epoch to settle account-owned work, but must pass the native UID/generation fence; presentation still requires its current authority/visit.
2. Atomically persist the original pending command **plus its validated terminal proof** in the same encrypted envelope, retaining original payload. No local settlement is reported before this durable write succeeds. Storage failure/ambiguous acknowledgement remains unresolved; reread the committed envelope on restart instead of overwriting it.
3. Under the same native ownership lock, verify the matching durable proof, then atomically retire just that pending command, retaining one bounded `lastSettledCommand` proof in that same envelope. This is a commit marker for the existing slot, not another journal/queue/history system. Failed retirement leaves the durable proof recoverable. A subsequent command cannot replace an unresolved slot; later successful settlement may replace this bounded last-proof marker. Resolved payload compaction follows verified settlement, never a discard of unknown work. No retention duration is selected.
4. Root/process recreation first validates storage, resumes proof-based retirement if step 2 committed, and only then publishes the settled state or attempts clean departure. A crash after step 3 cannot reissue the old command. No second network request is needed just to erase an already durably validated decision. Duplicate/late business and resolution callbacks compare exact pending identity and cannot clear a newer slot or overwrite a different proof.
5. The existing [clean-departure coordinator](../../shared/src/commonMain/kotlin/com/delminiusapps/tillfailure/identity/CleanDeparture.kt) can treat this validated retired proof as resolved state, while continuing to block on every unresolved command/journal/upload or storage ambiguity. Preserve freeze → prove clean → drain → marker → fence/dispose → Auth sign-out → terminate/cleanup → pin/marker completion. Another UID never receives a proof/payload. No cache clearing, account hot switch or Auth sign-out is a resolver side effect.

Do not synthesize an old invitation/onboarding/profile UI reply from the minimal outcome. `applied` shows “the original request completed”; fenced/conflict shows “the original request was not applied.” Disclose that Resolve can foreclose an uncommitted request and require explicit intent for this action; ordinary Retry continues to mean original business submission where authorized. This forecloses one uncommitted command identity; it never cancels an existing invitation, program or booking, or undoes committed effects. Stop issuing new local business retries once Resolve starts, but rely on the server fence for other devices/already in-flight calls. Resolving a lost create/resend response returns no token and performs no delivery; fresh invitation management needs normal authority.

From AccessLost/LockedRecovery, offer resolution for the original command when identity/binding permit. Show pending, terminal-result/storage-settlement, retryable unavailable and identity/binding-required states separately. A valid proof never opens a protected shell or updates cached profile/roster state. Scope loss clears presentation and fences navigation effects, including already-received effects; it does not destroy account-owned settlement. Local failures and older unsupported records remain locked with honest guidance.

## Smallest prerequisite slice and rollout

The 2026-10-21 implementation instruction authorizes this prerequisite sequence:

1. **Server prerequisite:** one guarded resolver export; explicit per-kind descriptor/hash/receipt adapters for the six implemented protected mutations; shared consumed-key/fence guard audit for all `lifecycleCommands` writers; coverage generation and finite admission controls. Preserve normal mutation authorization and existing receipt formats. Do not add revocation execution, role grants, resource read grants or general repair tooling.
2. **Shared/native prerequisite:** extend the same protected slot with binding/durable proof and new-key generation for new commands, acceptance digest capture, exact resolver DTO/transport, two-stage settlement and existing recovery entry. Implement both Android and Swift boundaries. Preserve legacy pending commands without rekeying/replacing them. No generic multi-command framework or separate UI history is required.
3. **Emulator compatibility proof:** isolated create-only fixtures and preserved existing exports/partitions. Test old writers/clients as well as new ones, close the required matrix below, then record the exact coverage generation/dataset provenance. No reset/reseed to make an absent receipt look safe. Restarts preserve receipts/fences/generation. A damaged or reset dataset disables absence terminalization rather than authorizing it.
4. **Separate revocation slice:** only after prerequisite acceptance, add the seventh descriptor/receipt adapter, exact approved membership transaction and UI. Prove loss-of-membership own resolution end to end before enabling the action. Existing global disable/unavailable-identity, damaged storage and legacy evidence gaps remain separately gated, not silently accepted as finished revocation behavior.

Roll out server guard/adapters and retention protections first, then resolver-capable clients/new-key issuance, then enable the resolver in the controlled emulator configuration. An older handler that rejects a fence remains safe but cannot provide its minimal outcome; an older client can require upgrade to settle. No schema-2 fence may be mistaken for a schema-1 success. Rollback may hide the new action but must retain fences/receipts and fail-closed guards; restoring older unguarded writers or deleting barriers is not a rollback strategy. Release/production hosts remain unconfigured for these operations. Production policy, migration, credentials, infrastructure and deployment approval are not supplied by local proof.

## Remaining decisions and explicit limits

- **Approved permission and protocol (2026-10-21):** active global identity may retrieve its own exact minimal outcome without workspace authority, and may atomically foreclose its own uncommitted key. This is distinct from approving revocation, privileged override or discarding unknown work. Josip approved this exact permission, fence and durable-proof protocol on 2026-10-21.
- **Approved initial compatibility scope (2026-10-21):** six current variants, then revocation; new generation covers absence only with proven intact history; supported legacy receipts project, while missing legacy acceptance binding/history and missing/disabled global identity stay locked. If those cases must also resolve before revocation, expand their identity/migration design first; do not relabel them as solved.
- **Retain broader gates:** final production retention/key retirement, backup provenance, recovery for deleted/disabled identity, damaged keys/storage, legal/privacy/support/export/discard and operational overrides remain open. No retention duration, deletion, forced repair or account restoration is approved. Service unavailability can delay resolution; no online protocol can guarantee an outcome while its authority/storage service is unavailable.

Implementation readiness: **emulator prerequisite implemented for review; revocation remains gated on its review and verification.** The approval is recorded in the local planning commit; implementation evidence belongs in the prerequisite report. Remaining legacy, identity and storage locks stay explicit; the report does not claim full production or interaction acceptance.
